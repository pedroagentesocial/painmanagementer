Headaches are so common that many people treat them as normal. But according to the World Health Organization (WHO), **headache disorders affect about 40% of the population** — roughly **3.1 billion people in 2021** — and they are more common in women than in men.<sup>[1]</sup>

Migraine in particular has a huge impact: in the 2021 Global Health Estimates it was **the third-highest cause of disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) worldwide** among neurological conditions, behind only stroke and neonatal encephalopathy.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Be careful with painkiller overuse

One fact that surprises many patients: **taking painkillers too often can make headaches worse**. This is called medication-overuse headache, and according to the WHO it may affect up to 5% of some populations.<sup>[1]</sup> That's why it's important for a professional to review what you take and how often.

## What helps, according to the WHO

The WHO notes that **education about medication overuse, migraine triggers and lifestyle changes is highly effective**. Measures that often bring relief include:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Limiting alcohol
- Keeping regular **sleep** and **exercise** schedules
- Eating healthily
- Staying well **hydrated**
- Keeping a **headache diary** to spot patterns and triggers

For migraine, the WHO says taking a pain reliever **at the first sign of an attack** (such as a visual aura) can help prevent the headache.<sup>[1]</sup> The main medication groups are pain relievers, anti-nausea drugs, specific anti-migraine medications and preventive medications.<sup>[1]</sup>

## What the CDC says about migraine attacks

The CDC's 2022 clinical guideline lists **triptans, NSAIDs, triptans combined with NSAIDs, antiemetics, dihydroergotamine and acetaminophen** as established treatments for episodic migraine attacks, along with gepants and lasmiditan. It **does not recommend opioids** for this use.<sup>[2]</sup>

> No medication should be taken without medical advice: each one has contraindications and side effects.

## When to see a doctor

Ask for an assessment if:

- You get headaches **often**, or more and more frequently.
- You need **pain relievers on many days of the month**.
- The pain makes you **miss work or cancel plans**.
- You've noticed a **change** in your usual headache pattern.

According to MedlinePlus, you should **tell your provider about sudden, severe headaches**, and **get help right away** for a headache **after a blow to the head** or one that comes with **a stiff neck, fever, confusion, loss of consciousness, or eye or ear pain**.<sup>[3]</sup>

> An accurate diagnosis of your headache type is the first step to choosing the right treatment — and to no longer living in fear of the next one.
