Getting up from a chair, sleeping on your side or walking the way you used to has become harder. Hip pain has many possible causes, and the NHS makes an important point: **don't try to diagnose it yourself**.<sup>[1]</sup> This guide helps you get oriented and know when to ask for help.

## What could be causing your pain?

<table>

<tr><th>How it shows up</th><th>Possible cause</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Over 45, pain that is worse when walking, and stiffness after moving</td><td>Osteoarthritis</td></tr>
<tr><td>Pain and stiffness that are worse after not moving, such as when you wake up</td><td>Rheumatoid arthritis</td></tr>
<tr><td>Pain that may spread to the thigh and is worse when moving or lying on your side</td><td>Bursitis</td></tr>
<tr><td>Pain, swelling or bruising after intense or repetitive exercise</td><td>Sprain, strain or tendonitis</td></tr>
<tr><td>Severe pain and being unable to walk after a fall or injury</td><td>Hip fracture</td></tr>
<tr><td>A hot, swollen hip with a high temperature or chills</td><td>Joint infection (septic arthritis)</td></tr>

## Hip osteoarthritis is on the rise

According to the Global Burden of Disease 2021 study, published in *The Lancet Rheumatology*, cases of hip osteoarthritis are projected to **rise 78.6% from 2020 to 2050**.<sup>[2]</sup>

For hip osteoarthritis, the CDC's 2022 clinical guideline lists **exercise (aerobic, aquatic or resistance)** and **manual therapy** as non-drug therapies.<sup>[3]</sup>

The NHS also mentions painkillers such as acetaminophen (paracetamol) or ibuprofen, as gel or tablets.<sup>[1]</sup> Always follow the label or your doctor's directions.

## When to see a doctor

Book an appointment if:<sup>[1]</sup>

- The pain **stops you from doing normal activities or affects your sleep**.
- It is **getting worse or keeps coming back**.
- It **hasn't improved after 2 weeks** of home care.
- **Stiffness lasts more than 30 minutes** after waking.

## Urgent warning signs

Get **urgent medical care** if you have:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Severe pain that **started suddenly** without a fall or injury
- A **swollen, hot** hip
- **Changes in skin color** around the hip
- Pain with a **high temperature** and feeling generally unwell

And go to the **ER or call 911** if you have:<sup>[1]</sup>

- **Severe pain after a fall or injury**
- **Inability to walk or put weight** on the leg
- **Tingling or loss of feeling** in the hip or leg after an injury

The NHS advises **not driving yourself** to the emergency room.<sup>[1]</sup>

> If hip pain limits your movement or keeps you from sleeping, an assessment can identify the source and help you choose the right treatment.
