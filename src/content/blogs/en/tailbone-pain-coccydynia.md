The **tailbone**, or coccyx, is the small bone at the very bottom of the spine. It is made of **3 to 5 fused segments**, and several ligaments and muscles attach to it, including those that support the pelvic floor and help with bowel control.<sup>[2]</sup> When it hurts, doctors call it **coccydynia**. The pain often improves within a few weeks, but it can last longer.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Symptoms

Tailbone pain is usually a **dull ache** at the base of the spine, sometimes with sharp pains.<sup>[1]</sup> It is often worse:<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>

- When **sitting**, especially for a long time or after a long car ride
- When **standing up** after sitting
- When bending forward
- During bowel movements
- During sex

The pain can make it hard to sleep and to get through daily activities.<sup>[1]</sup>

Sometimes no cause is found.<sup>[1]</sup>

## What you can do at home

The NHS recommends:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Sitting with **good posture and support for your lower back**, and considering a specially designed **coccyx cushion**
- **Avoiding sitting for long periods**
- Lying on your side to take pressure off the tailbone
- Applying an ice pack wrapped in a towel for **20 to 30 minutes**; a heat pack can be used for longer
- Taking anti-inflammatory painkillers such as ibuprofen
- Trying **pelvic floor exercises**
- Using a stool softener if the pain gets worse during bowel movements

HSS adds practical tips: choose a **wedge cushion with a cutout** for the tailbone, avoid seats that are too soft, and consider acetaminophen or a topical diclofenac gel if needed.<sup>[2]</sup>

If the pain doesn't settle on its own, a doctor may order an **X-ray** to look for a fracture or differences in the shape of the bone.<sup>[2]</sup>

## When to see a doctor

The NHS advises seeing a doctor if the pain hasn't improved after **a few weeks** of home treatment, if it affects your daily activities, or if you have a **high temperature** along with tailbone pain and pain somewhere else, such as the belly or lower back.<sup>[1]</sup>

HSS recommends calling a doctor **right away** if you have:<sup>[2]</sup>

- Tingling, numbness or weakness in one or both legs
- A sudden increase in pain or swelling
- Skin changes or fluid draining from the crease of the buttocks
- Prolonged constipation, or trouble controlling your bladder or bowels

> If sitting has become something you dread and a cushion hasn't been enough, an assessment can find what is behind the pain and whether physical therapy or an injection could help.
