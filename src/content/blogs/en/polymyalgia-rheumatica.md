Getting out of bed has become a struggle. Your shoulders and hips are so stiff that combing your hair or getting up from a chair takes effort, and it all started within a few weeks. If you're over 65, this could be **polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR)**, a condition that causes **pain, stiffness and inflammation** in the muscles around the **shoulders, neck and hips**.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Who gets it

According to the NHS, PMR is linked to age: **most people diagnosed are over 65**, it's **very rare in people under 50** and it's **more common in women than men**.<sup>[1]</sup> The cause isn't known; a mix of genetic and environmental factors is thought to be involved.<sup>[1]</sup>

## The warning signs you shouldn't ignore

The NHS notes that **up to 1 in 5 people with PMR** develop **temporal arteritis** (also called giant cell arteritis), an inflammation of the arteries in the head and neck.<sup>[1]</sup> Get medical advice **right away** if you have:<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>

- A **new or frequent headache** that comes on suddenly, sometimes with a sore or tender scalp
- **Jaw or tongue pain** when eating or talking
- **Vision problems**, such as double vision or loss of vision in one or both eyes

If it isn't treated, it can lead to serious problems such as **stroke or blindness**.<sup>[1]</sup>

## How it is diagnosed

See a doctor if pain and stiffness **last more than a week**.<sup>[1]</sup> Diagnosis can be difficult because the symptoms look like other conditions, such as **rheumatoid arthritis**, which need to be ruled out first.<sup>[1]</sup>

Steroids can have side effects, such as weight gain, high blood pressure, **weaker bones (osteoporosis)**, stomach ulcers and a higher risk of infections. About **1 in 20 people** have changes in mood, and these should be reported to a doctor promptly.<sup>[3]</sup> If you take steroids for more than 3 weeks, you should be given a **steroid card** to carry at all times.<sup>[3]</sup>

## Follow-up

The NHS describes check-ups **every few weeks during the first 3 months**, and then at least **every 3 months during the first year**, sometimes with blood tests to measure inflammation.<sup>[3]</sup> PMR often improves after treatment, although it can come back.<sup>[3]</sup>

> If you're over 50 and have had stiffness in your shoulders and hips for more than a week, especially in the morning, an assessment can help find the cause and start treatment early.
