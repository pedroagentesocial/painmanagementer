It starts as an odd burning or tingling on one side of your body. A few days later, a blistering rash shows up in a stripe. That's **shingles**, or **herpes zoster**, and its most common complication is nerve pain that can stay for **months or even years** after the skin heals.<sup>[2]</sup>

## What it is

According to the CDC, shingles happens when **the chickenpox virus reactivates** in someone who has already had chickenpox. The virus stays "asleep" in the nerves for years.<sup>[1]</sup>

- **About 1 in 3 people** in the United States will get shingles in their lifetime, and an estimated **1 million people** get it each year.<sup>[1]</sup>
- More than **99%** of Americans born before 1980 have had chickenpox, even if they don't remember it.<sup>[1]</sup>
- The risk **goes up with age** and in people with a **weakened immune system**, whether from illness or from medications such as steroids.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Postherpetic neuralgia

This is pain that **stays where the rash was** after the rash has cleared. The CDC describes it as long-term nerve pain that can be **extremely painful** and get in the way of daily life.<sup>[2]</sup>

- About **10% to 18%** of people who get shingles develop it.<sup>[2]</sup>
- The risk **increases with age**: in older adults the pain tends to be more severe and last longer. It is rare in people under 40.<sup>[2]</sup>

Other possible complications include **vision loss** when the eye is involved, skin infection and, very rarely, pneumonia, hearing problems or brain inflammation.<sup>[2]</sup>

## Why it pays to see a doctor early

**Antiviral** treatment (acyclovir, valacyclovir or famciclovir) helps the sores heal faster and eases acute pain. According to the CDC, **it works best when started within 72 hours** of the first symptoms.<sup>[3]</sup> If the rash is on your face or near your eye, get care right away to protect your vision.<sup>[3]</sup>

In adults 50 and older, the vaccine is **91% effective at preventing postherpetic neuralgia**.<sup>[4]</sup> It's also recommended for people who **have already had shingles**. The most common side effects, such as a sore arm, tiredness or headache, usually last **2 to 3 days**.<sup>[4]</sup>

## What to do if the pain won't go away

If weeks after your skin has healed you still have burning, stabbing pain or pain when clothing brushes the area, you don't have to just put up with it. Nerve pain has specific treatments, and an assessment helps choose the right one.

> If you had shingles and the pain is still there after the rash is gone, an assessment can help you control the nerve pain and get back to your daily life.
