You wake up, try to open your hand and one finger stays bent. When it finally straightens, it does so with a **click or a pop**, and the base of the finger hurts. This is typical of **trigger finger**, a condition in which a finger or thumb **gets stuck in a bent position**. It can get better on its own, but sometimes it needs treatment.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Why it happens

The tendons that bend your fingers run through a series of "pulleys" that keep them close to the bone. In trigger finger, the **tendon thickens** or develops a small nodule, and the pulley at the base of the finger (the **A1 pulley**) can also thicken and narrow. Instead of gliding smoothly, the tendon **catches**.<sup>[2]</sup> The AAOS explains that in most cases **there is no clear cause**.<sup>[2]</sup>

## Symptoms

- The finger or thumb **catches in a bent position** and clicks or pops as it straightens<sup>[1]</sup>
- Pain in the finger, thumb or palm, and a **tender lump** at the base of the finger on the palm side<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- Stiffness, or needing to press on the finger to straighten it<sup>[1]</sup>
- Symptoms that are **worse in the morning** and after hand activities<sup>[1]</sup>

## What you can do at first

The NHS explains that trigger finger can go away without treatment. In the meantime, it recommends **cutting back on the activities that make it worse** and taking painkillers such as ibuprofen.<sup>[1]</sup> See a doctor if symptoms don't improve or are affecting your daily life.<sup>[1]</sup>

The AAOS notes that injections are **less likely to work** in people with **diabetes**, long-standing triggering or trigger thumb, although they can still help. In people with diabetes who use insulin, blood sugar may rise for about **10 to 14 days** after the injection, so it should be monitored closely.<sup>[2]</sup>

## Recovery after surgery

Fingers are usually moved **right after the procedure**. The incision usually heals within a few weeks, but swelling and stiffness can take **4 to 6 months** to go away; most people feel fully recovered at about **6 months**. Complications are rare.<sup>[2]</sup>

> If a finger locks, clicks or hurts every morning and it isn't getting better, an assessment can help you choose the treatment that gets your hand working normally again.
