It isn't wear and tear from aging. **Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)** is a **chronic autoimmune disease**: the immune system **attacks the body's own tissues**, mainly the joints.<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup> According to the CDC, it mostly affects the joints of the **hands, wrists and knees**.<sup>[1]</sup>

## How to tell it apart

Two features help recognize it, according to NIAMS:<sup>[2]</sup>

- **It's symmetrical:** if it affects one knee or hand, it often affects the one on the other side too.
- **Morning stiffness:** joint stiffness that **lasts longer than 30 minutes**, typically after waking up.

Other common symptoms include:<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>

- Pain, tenderness, swelling, warmth and redness in **more than one joint**
- **Fatigue**, weakness and occasional low-grade fever
- Weight loss or loss of appetite

The disease alternates between **flares**, when symptoms suddenly appear or worsen, and periods of **remission**, when they improve or go away.<sup>[1]</sup>

It isn't known what makes the immune system turn against the joints; researchers point to a combination of **genes, environmental factors and sex hormones**.<sup>[2]</sup>

## Why treating it early matters

Left uncontrolled, RA can cause **long-term pain, swelling, balance problems and deformed joints**, and it can also **affect the lungs, heart and eyes**.<sup>[1]</sup>

The CDC stresses **early diagnosis and treatment** by a **rheumatologist**, because it helps **prevent joint damage**.<sup>[1]</sup> According to NIAMS, the goal of treatment is to **control the disease** until it reaches remission or near remission, with few or no signs of disease activity.<sup>[2]</sup>

## How it is treated

Treatment is based on **disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs)**, which slow progression, control joint swelling and prevent deformity. If the first treatment doesn't work, doctors may prescribe **biologic DMARDs**.<sup>[1]</sup>

The American College of Rheumatology's 2021 guideline includes **44 recommendations** on these drugs and stresses that they are **not prescriptive**: decisions should be made **together with the patient**, based on their values, goals, preferences and other health conditions.<sup>[3]</sup>

> No medication should be taken without medical advice: each one has contraindications and side effects.

## What you can do

RA **has no cure, but it can be managed**. The CDC recommends:<sup>[1]</sup>

1. Staying **physically active**.
2. Keeping a **healthy weight**.
3. **Avoiding joint injuries** and repetitive motions.
4. **Quitting smoking**.

> If you have pain and swelling in several joints with morning stiffness, an early assessment can make a real difference in protecting your joints.
