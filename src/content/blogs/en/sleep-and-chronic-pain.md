Anyone living with chronic pain knows nights can be the hardest part. The NIH note that chronic pain **may be accompanied by disturbed sleep**, along with anxiety, depression and difficulty moving around.<sup>[1]</sup> But the link also runs the other way: poor sleep can change how the brain processes pain.

## What we know: poor sleep amplifies pain

A University of California, Berkeley study published in 2019 in the *Journal of Neuroscience* compared healthy adults' response to pain after a normal night and after a night without sleep. It found that sleep deprivation:<sup>[2]</sup>

- **Increased activity** in the brain areas that sense pain (the somatosensory cortex).
- **Reduced activity** in regions that help regulate it.
- **Lowered the pain threshold**: a heat stimulus started to hurt at lower temperatures.

## What is still unclear

The evidence in **healthy** people is stronger than in people **with chronic pain**. A 2022 systematic review of 31 studies found moderate-to-limited evidence that fragmented sleep increases pain sensitivity in healthy people, but only **very limited evidence** that short sleep worsens spontaneous pain in people who already have chronic pain. The authors concluded that the effect in this group **remains uncertain**.<sup>[3]</sup>

In other words: sleeping well probably helps, but it is not a cure for pain on its own.

## A therapy with evidence behind it: CBT for insomnia

The NIH define **chronic insomnia** as sleep problems **3 or more nights a week lasting more than 3 months**, and list a regular sleep schedule, **cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I)** and medicines among the treatments.<sup>[5]</sup>

In short: CBT-I **clearly improves sleep**, with a **small effect on pain**, and it also improved depressive symptoms at the end of treatment. It had no significant effect on anxiety or fatigue.<sup>[4]</sup> That's why it works best **as part of a plan**, not as the only treatment.

## Habits the CDC recommends

The CDC recommends that adults aged 18 to 60 get **7 or more hours** of sleep, and offers these tips:<sup>[6]</sup>

1. Go to bed and get up **at the same time every day**.
2. Keep your bedroom **quiet, relaxing and cool**.
3. Turn off **electronic devices at least 30 minutes** before bed.
4. Avoid **large meals and alcohol** before bedtime.
5. Avoid **caffeine in the afternoon or evening**.
6. **Exercise regularly** and eat a healthy diet.

> If pain keeps you up at night and fatigue makes the pain worse, an assessment can help you address both at the same time.
