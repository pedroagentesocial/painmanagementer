Living with pain every day doesn't only wear out the body. The NIH note that chronic pain **may be accompanied by difficulty moving around, disturbed sleep, anxiety, depression and other problems**.<sup>[2]</sup> And the link runs both ways: according to the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), people with depression are at **higher risk of developing chronic conditions, including pain**, and people who have both **tend to have more severe symptoms of each**.<sup>[3]</sup>

## What the data shows

A study published in 2023 in the journal *Pain*, using data from nearly 32,000 adults in the U.S. National Health Interview Survey (NHIS 2019), found that:<sup>[1]</sup>

- About **12 million adults (4.9% of the adult population)** have both chronic pain and symptoms of anxiety or depression.
- **23.9%** of adults with chronic pain have symptoms of anxiety or depression, compared with **4.9%** of adults without chronic pain.
- Chronic pain is present in **most (55.6%)** adults with ongoing anxiety or depression symptoms.

## Signs to watch for

NIMH lists several symptoms of depression, including:<sup>[3]</sup>

- A persistent sad, anxious or "empty" mood
- Feelings of hopelessness or pessimism
- Loss of interest or pleasure in hobbies and activities
- Aches or pains, headaches, cramps or digestive problems without a clear physical cause

Not everyone has every symptom. And most importantly, according to NIMH, **depression is treatable, even if you have a chronic illness**.<sup>[3]</sup>

## Therapies backed by evidence

According to the NIH's National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH):<sup>[2]</sup>

- **Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)** is the prevailing psychological intervention for chronic pain.
- Both **mindfulness-based interventions** and **CBT** help **decrease pain intensity**, with no important difference between them.
- For chronic low back pain, **mindfulness-based stress reduction** is associated with a small improvement, and **yoga** and **tai chi** may also help.

The CDC's 2022 clinical guideline also includes **psychological therapy** and mindfulness among the non-drug options for chronic low back pain, and **CBT** for fibromyalgia.<sup>[4]</sup> For depression, NIMH says treatment typically involves **psychotherapy, medication, or both**.<sup>[3]</sup>

## Asking for help doesn't mean it's "all in your head"

Getting psychological support when you have chronic pain **doesn't mean the pain is imaginary**. It means treating the whole person: body, sleep, emotions and the way you cope day to day. That's why the best results often come when pain management and mental health care work together.

> **If you are in crisis** or thinking about harming yourself, in the U.S. you can call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), available 24/7, free and confidential. Text and chat services are also available in Spanish.<sup>[5]</sup>

> A comprehensive plan that combines pain treatment and psychological support can help you regain not just mobility, but quality of life.
