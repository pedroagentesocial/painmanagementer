It hurts to pick up your baby, open a jar or turn a key, and the pain sits right at the **base of the thumb, on the side of the wrist**. That is the classic picture of **De Quervain's tenosynovitis**, a condition in which the tendons that move the thumb become irritated inside a narrow tunnel in the wrist.<sup>[1]</sup>

## What is going on in the wrist

Two tendons run side by side through a tunnel on the thumb side of the wrist: one moves the thumb away from the hand and the other helps straighten it. They are wrapped in a slippery lining (the synovium) that lets them glide. In De Quervain's, the tendons become irritated and the **sheath thickens**, so they no longer glide freely; the friction causes pain.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Symptoms

- **Pain on the thumb side of the wrist**, which can start gradually or suddenly and spread up the forearm<sup>[1]</sup>
- Pain that gets worse when you **grip, lift or twist** the wrist, or make a fist<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- Swelling on the thumb side of the wrist<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- A **catching or snapping** feeling when you move the thumb<sup>[1]</sup>
- Trouble pinching with the thumb<sup>[2]</sup>

## How it is diagnosed

The diagnosis is usually made from your history and a physical exam. A common test is the **Finkelstein test**: you tuck the thumb into the palm, wrap your fingers around it and bend the wrist toward the little finger. If this hurts along the thumb side of the wrist, De Quervain's is likely. X-rays or ultrasound are usually not needed, but they may be used to rule out other problems.<sup>[1]</sup>

Once you can move the wrist without pain, MedlinePlus suggests starting **light stretching**, such as gently squeezing a tennis ball.<sup>[2]</sup> If you have heart, kidney or liver disease, high blood pressure or a history of stomach ulcers or internal bleeding, check with your doctor before taking anti-inflammatories.<sup>[2]</sup>

The AAOS notes that people with **diabetes** may respond less well to injections and have a higher risk of complications after surgery. Most people do well with nonsurgical or surgical treatment, and surgical complications are rare.<sup>[1]</sup>

## When to see your doctor

Contact your doctor if the pain doesn't improve or gets worse, the wrist gets stiffer, numbness or tingling in the wrist or fingers increases, or your fingers turn white or blue.<sup>[2]</sup>

> If the base of your thumb hurts every time you grip or lift something, an assessment can confirm the cause and help you choose the treatment that gets your hand back to normal.
