A sharp pain in the front of your chest that gets worse when you take a deep breath or press on your ribs is frightening. Sometimes the cause is **costochondritis**: inflammation of the cartilage where the **ribs join the breastbone** (sternum). It is not dangerous and usually gets better on its own, but **chest pain always has to be checked first** to rule out a heart or lung problem.<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>

MedlinePlus stresses that because the symptoms can look like a heart attack, **any chest pain** is a reason to call for help or go to the emergency room right away.<sup>[2]</sup>

## What costochondritis feels like

- **Sharp pain** at the front or side of the chest, which can spread to the back or the stomach<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- Pain that gets worse with **deep breaths, coughing**, upper-body movement or lying down<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- **Tenderness** when you press where the ribs meet the breastbone<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- Less pain when you are at rest<sup>[2]</sup>

## What can trigger it

The cause is often unknown.<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup> It has been linked to:

- A chest injury<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- Hard exercise, heavy lifting or other **repetitive upper-body movements**<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- **Frequent coughing** or viral respiratory infections<sup>[1]</sup><sup>[2]</sup>
- Infections, including after surgery or from IV drug use, and some types of arthritis<sup>[2]</sup>

## How it is diagnosed and treated

The diagnosis is mostly based on your history and an exam in which the doctor presses on the rib and breastbone joints. A chest X-ray may be ordered if the symptoms are severe or don't improve, and tests may be done to rule out other causes such as a heart attack.<sup>[2]</sup>

Before taking anti-inflammatories, check with your doctor if you have heart, kidney or liver disease, high blood pressure, or a history of stomach ulcers or bleeding. People with liver disease should avoid acetaminophen.<sup>[2]</sup>

## How long it lasts

Costochondritis often improves within a few days or weeks, but it can take **up to a few months**.<sup>[2]</sup> The NHS notes that symptoms can last from a few weeks to several months.<sup>[1]</sup>

If you have already been diagnosed, contact your doctor if you have **trouble breathing**, a **high fever**, signs of infection around the ribs, pain that persists or gets worse despite pain medicine, or sharp pain with every breath.<sup>[2]</sup>

> If chest-wall pain keeps coming back or is getting in the way of your work and sleep, an assessment can confirm where it comes from and help you find a plan to control it.
