Pain that starts in the lower back or buttock and **travels down the leg** to the foot. That's how **sciatica** is usually described, and it happens when something **presses on or irritates the sciatic nerve**.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Symptoms

According to the NHS, sciatica causes **pain, tingling, numbness or weakness**, usually in the bottom, the back of one leg, the foot and toes. Also:<sup>[1]</sup>

- It can start **suddenly or gradually**.
- It often **gets worse when you move, sneeze or cough**.
- There may be back pain too, but it is **usually less severe than the leg pain**.

## Most common causes

The most common cause is a **slipped (herniated) disc**, which becomes more likely with age. Other causes include **spinal stenosis**, **spondylolisthesis** and **back injuries**.<sup>[1]</sup> NIAMS lists these same conditions among the mechanical or structural causes of back pain.<sup>[2]</sup>

## How long does it last?

Sciatica **usually gets better in a few weeks to a few months**, although it can last longer. It **can also come back**, even after you've recovered.<sup>[1]</sup>

## What helps recovery

The NHS recommends:<sup>[1]</sup>

- **Staying as active as you can** and keeping up normal activities.
- Doing **gentle exercise** and recommended sciatica exercises.
- Using **heat packs** to ease the pain.
- **Avoiding sitting or lying down for long periods**, even if moving hurts.

On painkillers, the NHS notes that **paracetamol (acetaminophen) is unlikely to help**, and suggests asking a pharmacist.<sup>[1]</sup>

For nerve-related pain, the CDC's 2022 clinical guideline lists certain **antidepressants and anticonvulsants**, and **capsaicin or lidocaine patches**, as nonopioid options.<sup>[3]</sup>

## When to see a doctor

The NHS advises seeing your doctor if:<sup>[1]</sup>

- The pain **hasn't improved after a few weeks** of home care.
- The pain is **getting worse**.
- The pain is **stopping you from doing normal activities**.

## Emergency warning signs

The NHS says to get **emergency care** (in the U.S., go to the ER or call **911**) if you have:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Sciatica in **both legs**.
- **Severe or worsening weakness or numbness in both legs**.
- **Numbness around the genitals or bottom**.
- **Difficulty starting to pee, being unable to pee, or losing control of peeing**.
- **Not noticing the need to poo, or losing control of bowel movements**.

The NHS warns these symptoms can signal a serious back problem that needs **prompt hospital treatment**, and advises **not driving yourself**.<sup>[1]</sup>

> If sciatica doesn't improve or keeps coming back, an assessment can identify what is irritating the nerve and which treatment makes sense for you.
