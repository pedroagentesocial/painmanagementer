Pain when chewing, a jaw that locks when you yawn, or discomfort that spreads to your face and neck. These can be signs of a **temporomandibular disorder (TMD)**. According to the NIH's National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research (NIDCR), TMDs are a group of **more than 30 conditions** that cause pain and problems in the **jaw joint** (the TMJ) and the **muscles that move the jaw**.<sup>[1]</sup>

## How common is it?

- TMD affects about **5% of adults in the U.S.**, although the figure varies by study.<sup>[1]</sup>
- It is **about twice as common in women** as in men, especially between **ages 35 and 44**.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Symptoms

The most common symptom is **pain in the chewing muscles or the jaw joint**. NIDCR also lists:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Pain that spreads to the **face or neck**
- Jaw **stiffness**
- **Limited** jaw movement or a jaw that **locks**
- **Painful clicking** or grating when opening or closing the mouth
- Ringing in the ears, hearing loss or dizziness
- A **change in how the upper and lower teeth fit** together

A reassuring fact: **clicking without pain is common, considered normal, and doesn't need treatment**.<sup>[1]</sup>

## What causes it?

Some TMDs come from a joint injury, but **the exact cause is often unclear**. Research points to a mix of **genetic factors, psychological and life stress, and how each person perceives pain**.<sup>[1]</sup>

And something important: according to NIDCR, **research does not support the belief that a bad bite or orthodontic treatment causes** these disorders.<sup>[1]</sup>

## How it is diagnosed

**There is no standard test** to diagnose TMDs. Diagnosis is based on your symptom history and an exam of the head, neck, face and jaw. Imaging (X-ray, MRI or CT) is sometimes ordered, and other conditions may need to be ruled out.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Treatment: start conservative

Many TMDs **are temporary**, and for many people **symptoms go away without treatment**. That's why experts recommend **starting with conservative treatments** and avoiding those that permanently change the joint, teeth or bite.<sup>[1]</sup>
