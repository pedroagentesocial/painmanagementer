You wake up with a numb hand, feel pins and needles in your fingers, or keep dropping things. These can be signs of **carpal tunnel syndrome**, which according to the NHS is caused by **pressure on a nerve in the wrist**, the median nerve.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Symptoms

MedlinePlus describes **numbness, tingling or burning in the fingers, especially the thumb, index and middle fingers**.<sup>[2]</sup> The NHS adds:<sup>[1]</sup>

- An ache or pain in the fingers, hand or arm
- **A weak thumb or difficulty gripping**
- Symptoms that **start slowly** and come and go
- Symptoms that are usually **worse at night**

Symptoms often affect **both hands** and usually start in the dominant hand. They may get worse at night if you sleep with your wrists bent.<sup>[2]</sup>

## Who is most at risk

According to MedlinePlus, **women are three times more likely** to have it than men, and it usually affects only adults.<sup>[2]</sup>

## How it is diagnosed

Your doctor reviews your medical and family history, does a physical exam and, if needed, orders tests such as **lab work, X-rays, electrical studies of nerves and muscles, or an ultrasound**.<sup>[2]</sup>

MedlinePlus stresses that **it's easier to treat early on**, so it's best to start treatment as soon as possible.<sup>[2]</sup>

## Treatment, step by step

<tr><th>Step</th><th>What it includes (per the NHS)</th></tr>

<tr>
<td><strong>1. Home care</strong></td>
<td>A <strong>wrist splint at night</strong> to keep the wrist straight; you may need to wear it for <strong>up to 6 weeks</strong> before it starts to feel better. Cut back on activities that bend the wrist or require hard gripping. Hand exercises may help, although the evidence is limited.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>2. Steroid injection</strong></td>
<td>Reduces swelling around the nerve. It isn't always a permanent cure: symptoms can come back after a few months.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>3. Surgery</strong></td>
<td>If symptoms get worse and other treatments don't work. It <strong>usually cures carpal tunnel syndrome</strong>; it's a roughly 20-minute operation under local anesthetic with no overnight stay, and returning to normal activities can take about a month.</td>
</tr>

Pain relievers such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen may ease the pain in the short term, but they **don't treat the cause**.<sup>[1]</sup>

And some good news: it sometimes **gets better on its own within a few months**, especially when it's linked to pregnancy.<sup>[1]</sup>

## When to see a doctor

The NHS recommends seeing a doctor if:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Your symptoms are **getting worse or not going away**.
- **Home treatment isn't working**.

> If hand tingling keeps you up at night or makes you drop things, an early assessment can help you treat it before it progresses.
