Climbing stairs, getting up from a chair, going for a walk. Your knees are involved in almost everything you do, which is why you notice so quickly when one hurts. The pain can come from a recent injury or appear with no clear cause, and each case is handled differently.

## Knee osteoarthritis in numbers

One of the most common causes is osteoarthritis. According to the Global Burden of Disease 2021 study, published in *The Lancet Rheumatology*:<sup>[2]</sup>

- **595 million people** had osteoarthritis in 2020, about **7.6%** of the world's population.
- **The knee is the most common site**.
- Osteoarthritis is more common in women than in men.
- **High body-mass index** contributed to about **20%** of the disability burden from osteoarthritis.<sup>[2]</sup><sup>[3]</sup>

## What to do at home for recent pain

For recent knee pain, the NHS recommends:<sup>[1]</sup>

- **Putting as little weight as possible** on the knee and avoiding standing for a long time.
- Applying **ice** or a bag of frozen peas wrapped in a towel, **for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours**.
- Using pain relievers such as acetaminophen, or ibuprofen gel or tablets, following the label.

You should **start to feel better in a few days**. If it doesn't improve within a few weeks, see a doctor.<sup>[1]</sup>

## If it's osteoarthritis, moving helps

Rest works for a recent flare, but osteoarthritis is managed differently. For knee osteoarthritis, the CDC's 2022 clinical guideline lists **exercise (aerobic, aquatic or resistance)** and **weight loss**, plus **topical NSAIDs** for joints near the skin such as the knee.<sup>[4]</sup> The CDC adds that low-impact activity such as **walking, biking or swimming** can improve pain and function, and that **even a small amount of weight loss** can help people with overweight.<sup>[5]</sup>

> No medication should be taken without medical advice: each one has contraindications and side effects.

## Warning signs

The NHS advises getting **urgent same-day care** if:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Your knee is **very painful**.
- You **can't move it or put any weight on it**.
- It is **badly swollen** or has **changed shape**.
- It **locks, gives way or clicks painfully** (painless clicking is normal).
- You have a **very high temperature or chills with redness or heat** around the knee, which can signal an infection.

> If knee pain isn't improving or limits what you do each day, an assessment can identify the cause and help you choose the right treatment.
