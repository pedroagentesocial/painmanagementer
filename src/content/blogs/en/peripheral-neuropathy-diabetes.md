Burning, "pins and needles" tingling, or numb feet that feel worse at night. For many people with diabetes, these are the first signs of **peripheral neuropathy**, a type of nerve damage that, according to the NIH, **typically affects the feet and legs**, and less often the hands and arms.<sup>[1]</sup>

And according to the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK), **up to one-half of people with diabetes have peripheral neuropathy**.<sup>[1]</sup>

## Symptoms

NIDDK describes these symptoms, which **usually occur on both sides of the body** and **are often worse at night**:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Burning
- Tingling, like "pins and needles"
- Numbness
- Pain
- Weakness

## Why it matters: the risks to your feet

Losing feeling means a wound can go unnoticed. NIDDK warns that neuropathy can lead to **blisters and sores on the feet**, and that diabetes **makes these wounds hard to heal**. Other possible complications include:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Changes in the shape of the feet and toes and, rarely, **Charcot's foot** (damage to the bones and tissue of the foot)
- **Falls and fractures** from loss of balance
- **Grief, anxiety and depression** linked to chronic pain

## How the pain is treated

NIDDK says your doctor may prescribe **antidepressants, anticonvulsants such as gabapentin and pregabalin, and lidocaine creams or patches**, noting that they **ease pain but do not change the nerve damage**. It also mentions **physical therapy**.<sup>[1]</sup>

The CDC's 2022 clinical guideline agrees on nonopioid options for neuropathic pain: **tricyclic, tetracyclic and SNRI antidepressants; selected anticonvulsants (pregabalin, gabapentin enacarbil, oxcarbazepine); and capsaicin or lidocaine patches**.<sup>[3]</sup>

> No medication should be taken without medical advice: each one has contraindications and side effects.

## Prevention: what is in your hands

According to NIDDK, the main way to prevent or slow the damage is to **manage your blood glucose, blood pressure and cholesterol**: staying close to your goals **can keep nerve damage from getting worse**.<sup>[1]</sup>

It also recommends:<sup>[1]</sup>

1. **Checking your feet every day** for wounds, blisters or changes.
2. **Taking good care of your feet** every day.
3. Having your **doctor examine your feet at least once a year**, or more often if you already have foot problems.

> If you have diabetes and notice burning, tingling or pain in your feet, an assessment can help you manage the pain and protect your feet before complications appear.
