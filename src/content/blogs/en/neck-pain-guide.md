Waking up with a "locked" neck, or ending the workday at the computer with tense shoulders... Neck pain is so common that MedlinePlus simply calls it **"very common."**<sup>[3]</sup> And the global numbers back that up.

## The numbers

According to the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study, published in *The Lancet Rheumatology* in 2024:<sup>[1]</sup>

- In 2020, neck pain affected **203 million people** worldwide.
- By 2050, **269 million cases** are projected — a **32.5%** increase, driven mainly by population growth and aging.
- It is more common in **women** (2,890 per 100,000) than in **men** (2,000 per 100,000).
- It peaks between **ages 45 and 74**.

## Most common causes

According to the NHS, the most common causes are:<sup>[2]</sup>

- **Sleeping with the neck in an awkward position**.
- **Poor posture**, for example sitting at a desk for a long time.
- A **pinched nerve**.
- Injuries such as **whiplash** from a traffic accident or fall.

MedlinePlus adds that the pain can also **come from the shoulder, jaw, head or upper arms**.<sup>[3]</sup>

## How long it lasts

The good news: **most neck pain only lasts a few weeks**.<sup>[2]</sup>

## What you can do at home

The NHS recommends:<sup>[2]</sup>

- Using a **low, firm pillow**.
- Applying **heat or cold** to the neck.
- Doing **gentle neck flexibility exercises**.
- **Avoiding a neck collar**: it is better to keep the neck moving unless a doctor advises otherwise.
- Over-the-counter pain relievers such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen (including ibuprofen gel), following the label or pharmacist's directions.

MedlinePlus notes that treatment depends on the cause, and that **surgery is rarely needed**.<sup>[3]</sup>

## When to see a doctor

The NHS advises seeing a doctor if:<sup>[2]</sup>

- The pain or stiffness **doesn't go away after a few weeks**.
- Painkillers **haven't worked**.
- You are **worried** about the pain.
- You have other symptoms, such as **pins and needles** or a **cold arm**, which could point to something more serious.

> If neck pain keeps coming back or limits your work and rest, an assessment can identify the cause and give you a plan to keep it from returning.
