A misstep off a curb or a bad landing from a jump, and your ankle rolls inward. According to the 2021 clinical practice guideline in the *Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy*, an **ankle sprain** is **the most common foot and ankle injury** people seek medical care for. And it's estimated that **only about half** of people who sprain an ankle see a professional.<sup>[2]</sup>

## How to recognize it

According to the NHS, it's likely a sprain if you have:<sup>[1]</sup>

- Pain, tenderness or weakness around the ankle
- **Swelling or bruising**
- Trouble **putting weight on or using** the foot normally

During those first days, the NHS advises **avoiding heat (hot baths, heat packs), alcohol and massage**. Once you can move without pain stopping you, **keep moving the ankle** so it doesn't get stiff.<sup>[1]</sup>

## How long does it take to heal?

**After 2 weeks, most sprains feel better.** The NHS recommends avoiding **strenuous exercise such as running for up to 8 weeks**, and warns that severe cases **can take months** to get back to normal.<sup>[1]</sup>

## When it's urgent

The NHS advises getting **urgent care** if the pain is severe or getting worse, swelling or bruising is large or growing, **you can't put weight on it or walk more than a few steps**, or you have a fever or chills.<sup>[1]</sup>

And go to the **ER or call 911** if:<sup>[1]</sup>

- You **heard a crack** when you got hurt
- The ankle **looks misshapen or at an odd angle**
- It is **numb or tingling**
- The skin looks **blue or gray**, or feels cold

> If you rolled your ankle and it isn't improving, or it has happened more than once, an assessment and a rehabilitation plan can help keep it from happening again.
